# Mesh simulator fixtures

## `juelich-2018crowdqueue-030.movements.gz`

Run 030 (corridor 5.6 m wide, 75 participants, 64.8 s) of the experiment "Crowds in front of bottlenecks from the
perspective of physics and social psychology", Wuppertal 2018, from the Jülich/Wuppertal pedestrian dynamics data
archive: <https://doi.org/10.34735/ped.2018.1> (page <https://ped.fz-juelich.de/da/doku.php?id=crowdqueue>). Authors:
Juliane Adrian, Maik Boltes, Stefan Holl, Anna Sieben, Armin Seyfried. The archive's content is licensed under
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); this file is a separately licensed work under that licence,
not part of the GPLv3 code beside it.

Results from the experiment: J. Adrian, A. Seyfried, A. Sieben, "Crowds in front of bottlenecks at entrances from the
perspective of physics and social psychology", J. R. Soc. Interface 17(165), 20190871 (2020),
<https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2019.0871>.

Changes made: the PeTrack trajectory `030_c_56_h0.txt` (sha256
`917ecaf20ae314d7f696db7473e79b7ac43bb14a7ed0166397ab3e3df30a5bc0`, from `trajectories_txt.zip`, sha256
`1918daa6c774325d8daeae31cd79da6b6cbfadeb77e8fefeffededfe979815e8`) was resampled from 25 fps to one waypoint every
0.5 s plus each pedestrian's first and last frame, shifted so every coordinate is at least 1 m from the origin, rounded
to the centimetre and written in BonnMotion's `.movements` format by

```sh
scripts/sim-trace.py 030_c_56_h0.txt juelich-2018crowdqueue-030.movements.gz
```

`TraceMobility` replays it; `FieldMeterTest` runs it through the radio end to end.

## `reference/*.tsv`

The contacts of three published Bluetooth traces, as their devices recorded them one side at a time ("A saw B"),
reduced by `scripts/sim-reference.py` to counts on a log grid: how many gaps and contact lengths lie at or below each
point. They hold no device, no time and no sighting of the traces themselves, which stay with CRAWDAD under its terms
(the Haggle README asks that the data not be redistributed and be used only for research and education).
`ReferenceTable` reads them; `FieldMeter` scores a run's virtual scanners against them.

- `infocom05.tsv` and `infocom06.tsv`: the iMotes carried at IEEE Infocom 2005 (Miami, 41 iMotes, Exp3 of CRAWDAD
  `cambridge/haggle` v. 2006-01-31) and Infocom 2006 (Barcelona, the 78 carried iMotes 21–98, Exp6 of v. 2009-05-29),
  downloaded from IEEE DataPort
  (<https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/crawdad-cambridgehaggle-v-2006-01-31>,
  <https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/crawdad-cambridgehaggle-v-2009-05-29>). J. Scott, R. Gass, J. Crowcroft,
  P. Hui, C. Diot, A. Chaintreau, CRAWDAD dataset cambridge/haggle. As its authors ask: A. Chaintreau, P. Hui, J. Scott,
  R. Gass, J. Crowcroft, C. Diot, "Impact of Human Mobility on Opportunistic Forwarding Algorithms", IEEE Transactions
  on Mobile Computing 6(6), 606–620 (2007).
- `reality.tsv`: the 89 participants' phones that both scanned and were registered, July 2004 to May 2005, from the
  `devicespan` table of CRAWDAD `mit/reality` v. 2005-07-01 (<https://doi.org/10.15783/C71S31>,
  <https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/crawdad-mitreality>). N. Eagle, A. Pentland, "Reality mining: sensing complex
  social systems", Personal and Ubiquitous Computing 10(4), 255–268 (2006). 152 runs stamped outside those months
  (clocks that had reset) are left out.

Archives used (sha256): `imote-trace3.tar.gz` `9c642ebc0b5bfdf3e1fc971406f04a14086f62e1377059f9f6ea39d4652f6d80`,
`Exp6.tar.gz` `fecf7b962e9894df1c323bf90c4b4c6cd66763cc00d2c2fdb8dc3f25edd25f46`, `realitymining.tar.gz`
`feab9bf80b32a6b20ef6d66763ef74e2664c1285e584a66bcf671138b44b4ac1`. Regenerated by

```sh
scripts/sim-reference.py --haggle <unpacked cambridge/haggle> --reality <dump-anonymized.sql>
```
