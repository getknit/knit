Knit is an end-to-end encrypted messenger that runs on the phones themselves, with no account to make and no server in the middle. Phones connect directly over Wi-Fi Aware and Bluetooth, and nearby phones pass each message along, so it can cross a crowd, a building or a campsite far beyond your own range.

PRIVATE BY DEFAULT
• A fresh install makes no network calls, so no server learns who you talk to, when, or from where. Anything that uses the internet stays off until you turn it on.
• There's no account, phone number or email. You add people by QR code or by link.
• Knit has no ads, analytics or tracking, and doesn't use Google Play Services.
• A crash report stays on your phone until you read it and decide to send it.

END-TO-END ENCRYPTED
Direct messages and group chats are end-to-end encrypted with forward secrecy, and so are delivery receipts and reactions. Every phone and relay that carries a message sees only ciphertext. Keys are made on your phone and held in Android's hardware-backed keystore, and a safety number or QR scan confirms who you're talking to. Links between phones on current versions are encrypted as well, so a radio nearby can't read what passes between them.

The public Nearby room is the one exception. It has no fixed set of recipients, so it isn't encrypted, and it never leaves the radio mesh.

RELAYS YOU CHOOSE, OR RUN YOURSELF
To reach a contact who is never in radio range, turn on Internet relays. Knit leaves a sealed copy on a relay, which hands it over when their phone next connects. Before anything is sent, a one-time consent sheet sets out the trade: a relay sees your IP address, when you send and roughly how much. It never sees your messages or photos, who you're talking to, or who else is in a group. Turn relays off and uploads stop at once, and sealed copies already on a relay expire within 48 hours.

Use any relay, or run knit-spool (AGPL-3.0-or-later), the software behind Knit's own relay, and hand it out as an invite link. Calls can go through a TURN server so the person you call never sees your IP address; the server itself sees both.

OFF THE GRID
• Messages wait on the phones carrying them until the person is back in range
• Voice calls phone to phone, and walkie-talkie calls that hop across the mesh
• Big files go phone to phone over Wi-Fi, with no network around
• Install offline hands Knit to a nearby phone, no store or internet needed
• Pair an optional Meshtastic LoRa radio and direct messages and the Nearby room reach kilometres instead of metres

PERFECT FOR
• Power cuts and emergencies when networks fail
• The backcountry, ships, flights and travel abroad
• Festivals and crowded venues where signal dies

FREE SOFTWARE, BUILT REPRODUCIBLY
Knit is GPL-3.0-or-later. F-Droid builds it from source and compares the result byte for byte with the developer's signed APK before it ships that APK, so the app you install is the code at github.com/getknit/knit.

ALSO IN KNIT
• Quoted replies, emoji reactions, @-mentions and text formatting
• Photos, GIFs, short videos, voice notes and files of any type
• On-device translation, and English speech to text, after a one-time model download
• Search, and an encrypted backup for a new phone
• On-device content filtering, blurred sensitive images and one-tap blocking
• Material You dynamic colors and dark mode

ABOUT THE PERMISSIONS
Nearby devices (location before Android 13) lets Android find peers over Wi-Fi Aware and Bluetooth LE and reach a paired radio. Location itself is read only when you tap Send location. INTERNET is declared because Android counts the local Wi-Fi Aware socket as network access, and because relays, link previews and model downloads use it once you turn them on.

Knit is funded by its supporters: github.com/sponsors/getknit, ko-fi.com/knit. Liberapay.com/zaventh supports its developer alone.

Requires Android 10 or newer, and works best when allowed to run in the background.
