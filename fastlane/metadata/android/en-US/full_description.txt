Knit is a messenger that works with no internet, no cell service, and no accounts. It connects directly to the phones around you over Wi-Fi Aware and Bluetooth, passing messages device to device.

When you send a message, nearby phones receive it and pass it onward to the phones around them — so your words hop across a crowd, a building, or a campsite, far past your own signal range. The more people using Knit nearby, the further it travels.

WHY KNIT
• No internet or mobile data required
• No account, phone number, or email to sign up
• No central server — nothing to shut down, monitor, or leak
• No ads, tracking, analytics, or Google Play Services
• Free software — anyone can read what the app does

PERFECT FOR
• Festivals and crowded venues where signal dies
• Travel, flights, ships, camping, and the backcountry
• Power outages and emergencies when networks fail
• Anywhere you just want to reach the people around you

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
Direct and group chats are end-to-end encrypted. Each message is sealed so only the people in the conversation can read it — every phone that carries it onward, and every relay, sees only ciphertext. Your keys are generated on your device, protected by hardware-backed Android security. Confirm who you're talking to with a safety number or a QR scan.

ABOUT THE PERMISSIONS
Knit asks for nearby-devices (and, on Android 12 and older, location) because that is what Android requires to discover peers over Wi-Fi Aware and Bluetooth LE. It declares INTERNET because Android classes the local socket over a direct Wi-Fi Aware link as network access, and because Internet relays and link previews use it once you switch them on. No account, no backend — a fresh install makes no network calls at all.

FREE SOFTWARE
Knit is released under the GNU General Public License v3 or later, and the full source is public at github.com/getknit/knit. Read the code that runs on your phone, or build the app yourself.

FEATURES
• Public "Nearby" room to broadcast to everyone in range
• Private 1:1 messages (end-to-end encrypted)
• Multi-member groups (end-to-end encrypted)
• Quoted replies, emoji reactions, and @-mentions
• Profiles with a name, status, and avatar
• Image sharing, including GIFs
• Typing and delivery indicators, unread badges
• Store-and-forward — messages wait and deliver when someone returns to range
• Voice notes and files of any type in direct and group chats
• Add a contact from a link when you're too far apart to scan a code
• Optional Internet relays, off until you turn them on (see below)
• Share the Knit app itself to a nearby phone, offline, with no app store

SAFER SPACES
On-device filtering screens out abusive text and explicit images, entirely offline with nothing sent anywhere. Sensitive content is blurred behind a tap to reveal, and any sender is one tap from blocked.

HOW IT WORKS
Knit runs two radios at once — Wi-Fi Aware and Bluetooth Low Energy — so it works on phones with only one of the two. Best allowed to run in the background. Requires Android 10 or newer.

OPTIONAL INTERNET RELAYS
To reach a contact who is never in radio range, switch on Internet relays: Knit parks a sealed copy on a small relay server, which hands it over the next time their phone connects. Off until you turn it on, behind a one-time disclosure of what a relay can see — your IP address, when you send, roughly how much — and cannot: your messages, who you talk to, or who is in a group. Choose your relays or run your own; the protocol and server are public.

Note: with relays off, Knit reaches people who are nearby, or within reach of the crowd between you. The public Nearby room is unencrypted by design — no fixed set of recipients — and is never uploaded.
